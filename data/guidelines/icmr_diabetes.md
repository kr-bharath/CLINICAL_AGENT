# Presentation
Common symptoms include polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, unexplained weight loss, non-healing wounds, paraesthesia, recurrent infections, and blurring of vision. Many adults are asymptomatic at diagnosis, so keep a low threshold for screening, particularly with a family history, overweight or obesity, or a history of hypertension.

# Screening thresholds (Asian Indian context)
Screen from age 30 onward, or earlier for adults with risk factors, using fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, or an oral glucose tolerance test. The Asian Indian phenotype is associated with higher visceral adiposity and insulin resistance at a lower BMI than other populations, so a lower screening BMI threshold (around 23 kg/m^2) is used.

# Management approach
Start with structured lifestyle modification and metformin for most newly diagnosed adults. Keep a low threshold for diagnosis and ensure regular follow-up to confirm glycemic targets are being met, escalating therapy promptly rather than waiting through repeated visits at an unmet target.

# Associated conditions
Actively screen for and manage common comorbidities alongside glycemic control, including hypertension, dyslipidaemia, obesity, and — given local disease burden — tuberculosis.

# Red-flag presentation
Severe diabetic ketoacidosis (altered sensorium, rapid breathing), recurrent hypoglycemia, or chronic diabetes-specific complications warrant referral to a higher centre rather than continued primary-care management alone.
