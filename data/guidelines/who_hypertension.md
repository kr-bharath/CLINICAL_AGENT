# Diagnosis threshold
Confirm hypertension in non-pregnant adults using repeated, properly measured blood pressure readings before starting any drug treatment. A single elevated reading should trigger repeat measurement, not immediate pharmacotherapy.

# When to start drug treatment
Start blood-pressure-lowering medication for adults with confirmed systolic blood pressure at or above 140 mmHg or diastolic at or above 90 mmHg, after lifestyle counselling has been offered. For adults with existing cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or high estimated cardiovascular risk, treatment may be considered from a somewhat lower threshold.

# Treatment target
Aim for a target blood pressure below 140/90 mmHg for most adults. A lower target may be appropriate for people who tolerate treatment well and carry additional cardiovascular risk factors.

# Choice of first-line therapy
Start with a thiazide or thiazide-like diuretic, an ACE inhibitor, an angiotensin receptor blocker, or a long-acting calcium channel blocker. Beta-blockers are generally reserved for specific compelling indications (such as coexisting coronary disease or heart failure) rather than routine first-line use.

# Monotherapy versus combination therapy
For adults whose blood pressure is substantially above target, consider starting with a two-drug combination, preferably as a single-pill combination, rather than always beginning with monotherapy and titrating stepwise. Single-pill combinations improve adherence.

# Follow-up interval
Recommend monthly follow-up until blood pressure is controlled, then every three to six months once the target has been reached and the regimen is stable.

# Task-sharing in primary care
Non-physician health workers, operating within a clear protocol, can initiate and titrate treatment for uncomplicated hypertension. This expands access to care in primary and community health settings, particularly in low-resource contexts.
