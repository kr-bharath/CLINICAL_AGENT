# Diagnostic criteria
Diagnose diabetes with a fasting plasma glucose at or above 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), a random plasma glucose at or above 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) together with symptoms, or an HbA1c at or above 6.5% — confirmed on a repeat test if the person is asymptomatic.

# Glycemic targets
Individualize the HbA1c target: generally below 7% for most adults, relaxed toward 7.5-8% for older adults or those with significant comorbidity or a high risk of hypoglycemia.

# First-line pharmacological therapy
Metformin is first-line therapy for type 2 diabetes unless contraindicated (for example, significant renal impairment), alongside structured lifestyle advice on diet and physical activity from the point of diagnosis.

# Treatment escalation
If glycemic targets are not met on metformin plus lifestyle measures, add a second agent (such as a sulfonylurea, or an SGLT2 inhibitor where available and appropriate) promptly rather than waiting through repeated visits at an unmet target.

# Cardiovascular and renal risk management
Manage blood pressure and lipids alongside glycemic control, given the substantially elevated cardiovascular risk in people with diabetes. Screen for albuminuria to identify and slow progression of kidney disease.

# Red-flag presentation
Symptoms suggesting diabetic ketoacidosis (rapid breathing, altered sensorium, persistent vomiting, fruity breath odour) or severe hypoglycemia require urgent referral rather than routine outpatient adjustment.
