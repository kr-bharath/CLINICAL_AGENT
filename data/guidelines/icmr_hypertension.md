# Blood pressure classification (India STW)
Normal: systolic below 120 mmHg and diastolic below 80 mmHg. Action: reassure, encourage healthy lifestyle.
Elevated BP: systolic 120-139 mmHg and/or diastolic 80-89 mmHg. Action: lifestyle modification only.
Hypertension Grade 1: systolic 140-159 mmHg and/or diastolic 90-99 mmHg. Action: pharmacological management plus lifestyle modification.
Hypertension Grade 2: systolic 160-179 mmHg and/or diastolic 100-109 mmHg. Action: pharmacological management plus lifestyle modification, typically starting with combination therapy.
Hypertension Grade 3 (severe): systolic 180 mmHg or higher, or diastolic 110 mmHg or higher. Action: urgent pharmacological management; evaluate for hypertensive emergency if accompanied by target-organ symptoms.

# Preferred drug classes
First-line options include ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and calcium channel blockers (CCBs). Fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) are encouraged to improve adherence in the Indian primary-care setting.

# Red-flag presentation
Severe hypertension accompanied by chest pain, breathlessness, altered sensorium, severe headache, or visual disturbance should be treated as a hypertensive emergency and referred immediately, rather than managed through routine outpatient titration.

# Task-sharing and follow-up
Non-physician health workers can support monitoring and adherence counselling under supervision. Review blood pressure control and reassess target-organ damage and risk factors periodically.

# Lifestyle component
Population-level measures apply at every grade alongside any pharmacotherapy: reducing salt intake, increasing physical activity, weight reduction, and tobacco and alcohol moderation.
